# Water-Saving Proposals and Public Accountability

**A forecast of lower water use raises a harder question: what would improve for the city?**  Satterthwaite asks us to judge urban environmental performance partly by the costs transferred to other people, ecosystems, and future generations (1997, pp. 1669–1670).  A projected reduction at one facility therefore leaves the wider public benefit to investigate.

My first memo asked how water commitments remain accountable when assumptions change.  I now focus on what connects a proposed improvement to the obligations governing a project.  I want to understand when a revised design changes what a developer must do, while keeping that legal question separate from evidence of environmental benefit.

I examine the Cerrillos Data Center’s environmental review in Chile between 2020 and 2024.  My research question is: How did Chile’s Second Environmental Tribunal distinguish the 2022 redesign screening from the original environmental approval in its 2024 groundwater review, and what legal consequences followed?

My research with Digital Emissions motivates my interest in how public decisions use environmental evidence.

## 1. Which course theories organize my inquiry?

**North, Satterthwaite, and Sen provide three conceptual perspectives; Lowe and colleagues provide a methodological precedent.**  Their different roles organize one investigation.

**North: institutions and incentives.**  Institutions include formal rules, informal constraints, and enforcement; organizations act within them.  Adjusting a project within existing rules differs from altering those rules (North, 1994, supplied reproduction, pp. 10–12).  I would examine which authority could change an obligation and whether compliance influenced conduct.  A recorded requirement alone cannot establish that mechanism.  I would look for a duty, an authority able to enforce it, and a documented decision responding to it.

**Satterthwaite: environmental performance.**  His framework extends evaluation across places and periods.  Easily measured indicators can crowd out consequential problems with sparse data (1997, pp. 1669–1670).  Facility efficiency therefore cannot represent the entire sustainability question.

**Sen: plural evaluation.**  Weighing different dimensions of well-being requires public reasoning beyond technical measurement (2000, pp. 18, 21).  My evaluative commitment is to examine development benefits and water access, including how affected people can challenge the criteria.

**Lowe: methodological bridge.**  The authors systematically examined policy content while excluding implementation from their study (2022, pp. e883, e890).  I would adapt that separation to the case, rather than treat their work as another theory.

## 2. How do these theories deepen my understanding of Cerrillos?

**Applying North.**  The judgment describes a proposed switch from water-based cooling towers to air-cooled equipment.  It reports that the 2022 screening found environmental assessment of the redesign unnecessary but could not amend RCA 127/2020, the original approval (Segundo Tribunal Ambiental, 2024, considerations 93–94, pp. 64–65).  General agency guidance likewise separates screening from execution authorization and modification of an approval (Servicio de Evaluación Ambiental, 2022, paras. 4–8, p. 2).

The court retained the original groundwater assessment for review, partially annulled the approval, and suspended its remaining effects pending complementary approval.  Reconsideration was to address climate-change effects on groundwater where applicable, considering any cooling modification (Segundo Tribunal Ambiental, 2024, operative provisions 1–2, p. 72).  North helps distinguish the legal constraint from evidence of a developer’s response.  The October 9 lecture similarly asked what sustains commitments as people and priorities change (Libertun de Duren, 2026, 45:58–48:56).

**Applying Satterthwaite.**  I would distinguish monthly withdrawals, seasonal groundwater levels, and household supply hours by neighborhood.  These are proposed indicators, not acquired observations.  Groundwater levels alone cannot establish recovery; rainfall and other users’ pumping could affect them.  A citywide supply average can also conceal differences among neighborhoods.

**Applying Sen.**  I would record employment and water-access claims separately, identify who selected the criteria, and examine objections and responses.  This exposes value judgments without imposing arbitrary numerical weights.  Silence in a judicial record cannot establish community agreement.

The Amazonia readings connect ecological functions with human well-being and reliable local evaluation data (Gauto Espinola & Llaguno Ribadeneira, 2025, pp. 157–161; Libertun de Duren, 2025, p. 173).  They inform evidence requirements without making engineered cooling nature-based.

**Applying Lowe’s approach.**  I would distinguish a commitment’s presence and specificity from evidence of implementation.  Table 1 links the conceptual questions to that method.  Neither detailed wording nor numerical precision establishes performance.

**Documentary case study.**  I would combine structured content analysis with chronological comparison.  North, Satterthwaite, and Sen guide what I examine; Lowe informs the coding procedure.  I would select 2020–2024 records addressing cooling, groundwater, or authority, applying identical criteria to supporting and contrary evidence.  Each assertion would retain its issuer, version, page, status, and dependency on other sources.  Selecting this revealing case cannot establish how frequently similar problems occur. I would retain decisive Spanish wording beside English paraphrases, flag disputed classifications, and seek qualified review.  Repeated accounts from one filing would remain one evidentiary chain.  Coding would record obligations, environmental boundaries, and represented interests separately.

**Competing explanations.**  Appropriate judicial review can coexist with deficient original assessment.  Another binding act incorporating the redesign before judgment could challenge an interpretation based on unchanged cooling requirements.  An unsuccessful search establishes only an access gap.  Technical or commercial motives for redesign require different evidence from the court’s reasons.  Chronology alone establishes neither motive nor unequal influence.

**Quantitative limits.**  Comparisons require compatible periods, service assumptions, weather, and units.  Invented ratios illustrate the difference between efficiency and totals: 20% less withdrawal per service unit with 50% more service gives 0.8 × 1.5 = 1.2, or 20% more total withdrawal.  This is arithmetic, not Cerrillos evidence.  Peak flow cannot establish annual volume; withdrawals differ from consumption.

The redesign request and attachment, complete screening determination, Resolution 463/2021, and standalone renunciation letter remain uninspected.  These gaps prevent a reliable technical comparison.  Implemented savings, aquifer recovery, net benefit, and an AI-driven cause remain unestablished in this computing-infrastructure case.

**Closing reflection.**  My first memo identified an accountability problem.  I now propose a method for testing how a technical proposal connects to governing obligations and identifying evidence that could change the interpretation.  That account could inform public scrutiny of resource decisions, while environmental benefits remain questions for further observation.
