## What Class 2 adds

My first memo asked how a city can reconsider a water commitment when its assumptions change. The September 11 discussion brings me back to the people who would make that reconsideration possible. In response to Emily and Jose, Professor Libertun raised lack of awareness as one possible explanation for overuse and asked how knowledge becomes action. Her account of a park-access project also showed why a mapped service deficit could leave residents' priorities unresolved (Class 2, 33:09 and 50:03).

I am keeping The Dalles as a provisional case, but I want to examine more than whether monitoring was required. The question is who could understand the information, question its meaning, and use it to reconsider a commitment.

## Decisions shaping the study

I will follow one documented assurance about water supply through the supporting study, agreement terms, and later public records. Starting with one claim should let me test whether those records actually concern the same water source, customers, and period. The executed agreement, supporting studies, and comparable operating observations remain outstanding in the current source register.

North and Ostrom guide different parts of this inquiry: the rules and incentives governing action, and the conditions that support cooperation. I would look for recorded communication, opportunities to question assumptions, and the authority and resources available to respond. Where a response is unclear, I will consider missing information, limited capacity, and a decision to retain the existing arrangement as possible explanations. Records of behavior would have to support any account of why people acted.

Lowe and colleagues leave implementation and performance tracking open for further study. Satterthwaite asks how human needs can be met while environmental capital is preserved. Together, they keep my evaluation focused on what matters to people and water sources: reliable service, affordability, and ecological conditions. An added quantity of supply could bear on those questions, but each outcome needs appropriate evidence (Lowe et al., 2022, pp. e890-e891; Satterthwaite, 1997, pp. 1680-1682).
