# Water-Saving Proposals and Public Accountability

## The problem and the pathway

A proposed reduction in a data center's water demand raises two questions: what would change environmentally, and what would change in the requirements governing the project? Public authorities need to distinguish those questions before relying on a redesign. My first memorandum examined accountability for water commitments as assumptions change. I now focus on regulatory accountability through environmental review, using the Cerrillos Data Center proceedings in Chile between 2020 and 2024.

My research question is: How did the Second Environmental Tribunal distinguish the legal effect of the 2022 consulta de pertinencia from RCA 127/2020 when it reviewed the Cerrillos Data Center's original groundwater assessment in 2024, and what legal consequences followed from that distinction?

Cerrillos is a computing-infrastructure case. My interest in AI governance motivates questions about evidence and accountability; it supplies no basis for attributing the redesign or dispute to AI workloads. The inquiry concerns how institutions evaluate changes to infrastructure that depends on environmental resources.

## What the records establish

The tribunal described a proposal to replace water-based cooling towers with air-cooled equipment. According to its account, the developer submitted a consulta de pertinencia, a request asking whether the proposed change required entry into environmental assessment. The court reported that the 2022 screening found such entry unnecessary, yet distinguished that determination from modification of RCA 127/2020, the original environmental qualification (Segundo Tribunal Ambiental, 2024, considerations 93–94, pp. 64–65).

The SEA's 2022 guidance states that a pertinencia response grants neither rights nor authorization to execute a project and leaves applicable environmental requirements and sectoral authorizations in place. It also states that this response cannot amend an RCA (Servicio de Evaluación Ambiental [SEA], 2022, paras. 4–8, p. 2). The court therefore retained the original groundwater assessment as an issue for review (Segundo Tribunal Ambiental, 2024, consideration 94, p. 65). Its distinction concerns the scope of an administrative act, rather than a finding that the proposed equipment lacked environmental value.

The judgment partially annulled the RCA and required reconsideration addressing climate change in the groundwater assessment, with an eventual cooling modification considered where applicable. It suspended the remaining effects and barred execution pending a complementary RCA (Segundo Tribunal Ambiental, 2024, operative provisions 1–2, p. 72). A later resolution acknowledged the holder's renunciation and declared that the project could not proceed under the original RCA's terms (Comisión de Evaluación de la Región Metropolitana de Santiago, 2024, operative provisions 1–3, p. 3). These are documented legal consequences. Their environmental effects require separate evidence.

## Why the distinction matters to sustainable cities

North treats formal constraints, informal constraints, and enforcement characteristics as components of institutions. He also distinguishes institutions from the organizations acting within them (North, 1994, supplied reproduction, pp. 10–11). His account directs attention to which authority could alter a requirement and how that requirement could affect conduct. Libertun de Duren's October 9 lecture similarly distinguished establishing an initiative from sustaining institutional commitments as people and priorities change (Libertun de Duren, 2026, 45:58–48:56). A durable mandate remains one condition to investigate; its existence does not establish adequate resources or enforcement.

Satterthwaite argues that cities' environmental performance includes the costs transferred to other people, ecosystems, and future periods. He warns that easily measured indicators can displace important problems with less available data (Satterthwaite, 1997, pp. 1669–1670). For Cerrillos, that framing prevents a projected reduction in one resource demand from becoming a complete sustainability judgment. Groundwater quantity, water quality, ecological condition, and consequences for water users require different evidence. Identifying affected households or unequal burdens would require observations beyond project proximity.

The assigned Amazonia section describes nature-based interventions through ecological functions and human well-being, while the volume's conclusion emphasizes reliable local data for evaluating promising initiatives (Gauto Espinola & Llaguno Ribadeneira, 2025, pp. 157–161; Libertun de Duren, 2025, p. 173). I use these passages to define what an environmental-benefit claim must specify. They neither classify the cooling redesign as nature-based nor establish benefits in Chile. North guides the institutional inquiry; Satterthwaite sets its evaluative boundary.

My evaluative starting point is that people affected by a resource decision should be able to examine the commitments and assumptions relied upon. The documents can reveal whose positions entered formal review; they cannot establish whether participation was representative or whether all affected interests received equal consideration.

## Method and rival explanations

I will conduct a documentary case study. The unit of analysis is a dated assertion about environmental demand or legal effect within an identified instrument. Each entry will record the issuer, document version, page or section, attributed claim, operative wording, qualification, and missing dependency. Separate fields will distinguish proposed design, projected resource effect, screening, binding requirements, implementation, and measured environmental change.

Lowe and colleagues offer a methodological precedent: they systematically coded policy content and its specificity, but expressly excluded implementation from their study (Lowe et al., 2022, pp. e883, e890). My legal-effect categories adapt that distinction to this case; they are not Lowe's original indicators. Spanish operative wording will remain beside English paraphrases in the evidence record. Ambiguous modality will remain unresolved until checked against the original passage.

The original redesign request and attachment, complete screening determination, Resolution 463/2021, and standalone renunciation letter remain uninspected for this comparison. I will attribute their descriptions to the court or later resolution. I cannot reconstruct equipment quantities, comparable demand forecasts, annual water use, or electricity tradeoffs from those descriptions. The absence of a retrieved document establishes an access limitation, rather than misconduct.

Ordinary screening and judicial review may have operated as intended. That explanation must be tested alongside deficient original assessment or a redesign insufficiently connected to binding requirements. Independent technical or commercial reasons may explain the proposal; unequal information, representation, or administrative capacity may explain aspects of participation or implementation. Documentary sequence alone cannot choose among those possibilities. Each requires evidence linking the proposed mechanism to a recorded action, including evidence that could weaken it.

## Contribution and next decision

The proposed contribution is a traceable account of why distinct instruments treated one proposed change differently. It identifies what must be checked before an environmental-improvement claim becomes a basis for public action. It establishes neither a new theory nor implemented savings, aquifer recovery, net environmental benefit, or representative community agreement. The next step is to verify each material interpretation against its original passage and revise it when contrary evidence appears. Accountability depends on knowing which commitment governs, who can act, and what evidence would establish the consequences.
