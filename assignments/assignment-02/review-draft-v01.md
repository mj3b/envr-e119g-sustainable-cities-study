# When a Redesign Does Not Replace an Authorization

## From a broad concern to a specific pathway

My first memorandum asked how public water commitments remain accountable when their underlying assumptions change. I now focus on regulatory accountability through environmental review. The case is the Cerrillos Data Center proceedings in Chile between 2020 and 2024. I will examine how public authorities distinguish a proposed technical change from a change in a project's legal position. This choice narrows both the pathway for action and the evidence needed to investigate it.

My research question is: How did the Second Environmental Tribunal distinguish the legal effect of the 2022 consulta de pertinencia from RCA 127/2020 when it reviewed the Cerrillos Data Center's original groundwater assessment in 2024, and what legal consequences followed from that distinction?

This is a computing-infrastructure case. My broader interest in AI governance motivates attention to evidence and accountability; it supplies no evidence that AI workloads caused this redesign or dispute. The immediate research problem concerns environmental review, institutional authority, and the grounds on which a project may proceed.

## The documentary puzzle

The tribunal described a 2022 proposal to replace water-based cooling with air-cooled equipment. It nevertheless rejected the contention that the water issue had lost its relevance. Its reasoning distinguished the pertinencia response from amendment of the original environmental qualification, RCA 127/2020 (Segundo Tribunal Ambiental, 2024, considerations 93–94, pp. 64–65).

SEA's own guidance describes pertinencia as an opinion on whether a project must enter environmental assessment. It states that the response neither grants execution rights nor relieves the proponent of applicable environmental requirements and sectoral authorizations (Servicio de Evaluación Ambiental [SEA], 2022, paras. 4–8, p. 2). This distinction gives the inquiry a precise object: the relationship between an administrative screening and an operative environmental qualification.

The court partially annulled the RCA and required reconsideration of the groundwater assessment with climate change addressed, taking an eventual cooling modification into account where applicable. It also suspended the unannulled portion and barred execution pending a complementary RCA (Segundo Tribunal Ambiental, 2024, operative provisions 1–2, p. 72). A later resolution acknowledged the holder's renunciation and stated that the project could not proceed under that RCA's terms (Comisión de Evaluación de la Región Metropolitana de Santiago, 2024, operative provisions 1–3, p. 3). These records establish legal consequences; they do not measure ecological recovery.

## A limited theoretical framework

North's account directs attention to rules and their operation. In his Nobel lecture, he treats formal constraints, informal constraints, and enforcement as components of institutions (North, 1993, section II). I use that distinction to ask what each instrument could change, who had authority to act, and what obligations remained. The framework does not establish that an agency lacked capacity or that a firm sought to evade review. Those explanations require their own evidence.

Satterthwaite's published abstract frames environmental performance in relation to sustainable development's social, economic, and political goals (Satterthwaite, 1997, abstract). Applied here, that framing keeps the study from treating a favorable design description as a complete sustainability judgment. My proposed assessment asks which resource pressures a decision addresses, which remain outside it, and whose interests the available record makes visible. I will distinguish the project's location from the geographic extent of any claimed water impact. Identifying affected households or unequal burdens would require additional evidence, rather than inference from proximity alone.

These readings perform different functions. North informs the institutional questions; Satterthwaite informs the evaluative boundary. Neither makes the judicial outcome proof of sustainable development. The cited Nobel lecture and published abstract are also distinct from the syllabus editions; their textual and pagination equivalence remains unverified.

## Research design and evidentiary tests

I will conduct a bounded documentary case study. The unit of analysis is a dated claim about environmental demand or legal effect, linked to the instrument in which it appears. For each material claim, I will record the author or authority, source version, page or section, precise wording, proposed interpretation, qualifying evidence, and missing dependency. The chronology will distinguish filing, determination, judicial disposition, and later renunciation. A sequence alone cannot explain motive.

The source-closure work limits what this design can establish. The original redesign submission, its principal attachment, the complete 2022 screening determination, Resolution 463/2021, and the standalone renunciation letter remain uninspected for this comparison. Consequently, I will attribute the court's descriptions to the court and will not reconstruct the redesign's exact equipment, operating assumptions, electricity implications, or water savings from those descriptions. The full technical before-and-after question remains outside the present draft.

The course reading by Blackman and Hoffmann provides a useful methodological discipline. Their theory of change is explicitly a set of hypotheses, while their health-benefit estimates require assumptions beyond the experimental outcomes (Blackman & Hoffmann, 2025, pp. 506, 513). For Cerrillos, I will similarly separate the proposed intervention, the institutional response, and the environmental outcome. I am borrowing that distinction, not their causal identification strategy or findings about Bogotá.

I will test the preferred interpretation against rivals. Ordinary screening and judicial review may have operated as intended. Alternatively, the original assessment may have been deficient, the redesign may have remained weakly connected to binding obligations, or technical and commercial considerations may have driven it independently. Unequal access to information, representation, or implementation capacity remains a further possibility. Each explanation needs evidence about its own mechanism. A missing document supports an access limitation, not a finding of institutional misconduct.

## Contribution and next decision

The intended contribution is a reconstruction of how distinct instruments handled one proposed environmental change. It can clarify what an official must verify before relying on a redesign claim: its evidentiary basis, its incorporation into operative requirements, and the separate evidence needed to establish implementation and effects. It cannot establish a new theory, a general failure of adaptive permitting, implemented savings, or community consensus.

The next decision is whether the record supports each sentence of that narrower account. I will preserve contrary findings and revise the question if a recovered original changes the relationship between the instruments. Public documentation should make that revision traceable. The practical question is who remains answerable for the environmental assessment when a developer presents a different design, and which record makes that responsibility effective.
