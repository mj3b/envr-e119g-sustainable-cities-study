## Examine the provisional commitment-assurance chain

Perplexity’s author-supplied critique suggested this organizing sequence. The memo uses it as six connected questions addressing a provisional commitment-accountability gap: the distance between authorization and the capacity to reassess. It is not a validated causal model or a finding that a local accountability gap exists.

| Link | Governing question | Evidence to seek |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Justification | What evidence and assumptions supported approval? | Dated studies, demand and supply assumptions, considered alternatives |
| Disclosure | What could affected people inspect and challenge? | Released materials, requests, access conditions and responses |
| Monitoring | Which conditions were measured over time? | Indicators, units, observation periods and measurement records |
| Trigger | What condition required review or intervention? | Defined thresholds, review dates and actual observations |
| Authority | Who had the power and responsibility to respond? | Executed terms, assigned duties, resources and continuity arrangements |
| Remedy | What corrective action was available and taken? | Response provisions, action records and subsequent observations |

The links can interact and operate through several institutions. Identify the responsible actor and period for each. A missing public record leaves a question unresolved; it does not establish that a safeguard was absent or that harm occurred. Evidence that all six links existed would still leave the adequacy of the allocation and the causes of outcomes open to evaluation.

## Test the case account and the governance standard

A reasonable favorable account is that officials assessed uncertainty, weighed competing uses, and negotiated infrastructure or conditions that improved reliable service while protecting other users and ecological conditions. Limited access to records would not by itself disprove that account.

A deeper challenge concerns the memo’s proposed standard. Good outcomes could occur with modest disclosure; extensive documentation could coexist with weak implementation or poor outcomes. Transparent reasoning, monitoring and corrective authority therefore need evaluation as possible means of protection. Their presence does not establish success, and their absence does not prove harm. Additional assessment and monitoring also consume resources. A fair investigation should ask what the proposed arrangements changed, at what cost, and compared with which feasible alternative.

| Test | Evidence to seek | What the result could change |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Was uncertainty examined before approval? | Dated projections, adverse scenarios, feasible alternatives, deliberations and conditions tied to identified risks | Documented examination would weaken an interpretation of unexamined risk. Missing records leave the matter unresolved. |
| Did an agreed safeguard operate? | Executed terms, named authority, monitoring results, a defined trigger and the documented response | A response record would provide evidence of operation. Check whether the action occurred and fulfilled the condition; documentation alone cannot settle that. |
| Did performance improve, and for whom? | Comparable periods and boundaries for withdrawals, consumption, service reliability, household costs and ecological observations; accounts from affected groups | Favorable findings should revise an adverse assessment. Weather, workload and other infrastructure changes remain competing explanations. A comparator alone does not establish causality. |
| What might the official measures omit? | Residents’ accounts, with dates, the conditions described and differing experiences; compare these with the measured record | Accounts could reveal omitted consequences or clarify what people value. They require interpretation and do not automatically establish a causal effect. |

Review question: What evidence would make me change my position, and would I apply the same evidentiary standard to favorable and adverse results?

These are proposed tests. No completed local causal evaluation, preregistration or validated case method is claimed.
