## 1 · The question worth carrying into the memo

**Proposed guiding question:** How should cities govern AI infrastructure when decisions are uncertain and the benefits and resource burdens fall unevenly?

The inquiry connects three dimensions through one decision: **who can authorize and correct it; whose opportunities and burdens count; which ecological conditions the commitment must respect.** Evidence must establish whether and how burdens are uneven in a specific case; the question does not establish that finding in advance.

## 4 · Test the interpretation fairly

| Proposed line of inquiry | Strong rival or correction | Evidence that would discriminate |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Public assurances may be difficult to evaluate independently | The authority may have held adequate technical evidence even where public access was limited | Dated studies, the decision packet, recipients, assumptions, and disclosure record |
| A resource commitment may shift risks across users | Infrastructure and enforceable terms may improve service for the wider community | Executed terms, monitoring and response records, compatible service and environmental observations |
| An aggregate benefit may conceal unequal consequences | The benefit may be broadly distributed, or trade-offs may have been addressed | Disaggregated benefits/burdens, affected-population boundaries, and evidence beyond a single participant |

These are competing possibilities, not findings. The available packet cannot establish subsequent water security, net environmental harm, or the causal effect of an agreement.
