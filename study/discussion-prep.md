# Discussion and oral defense

[Home](../README.md) / [Study](README.md) / Discussion preparation

**Purpose:** make an argument that can survive a fair objection and identify what would change it.

| Status | Boundary |
| :--- | :--- |
| Analyst-prepared prompts for practice | These are not instructor questions, student answers, or an assessment record |

## A four-part discussion note

| Prepare | Record |
| :--- | :--- |
| Claim | One precise statement and its evidence class |
| Support | Source, locator, and the inference connecting evidence to the statement |
| Objection | The strongest plausible alternative or limitation |
| Revision test | Evidence that would weaken, strengthen, or change the claim |

A peer example can open a research lead. Establish the underlying event independently before using it as evidence of effectiveness. See `P2-05` in the [claim registry](../cross-course/claims.json): the recorded classroom account does not verify its substantive corporate claim.

## Four course-grounded prompts

| Prompt | Starting evidence | Objection to take seriously |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Can formal approval explain how a resource condition operates? | `N-02`; `L1-01` | Informal practice, enforcement capacity, and incentives may change implementation |
| What would make a claimed benefit count as development? | `S-01`–`S-03`; `L1-03` | An aggregate gain can conceal distributional losses or contested weights |
| What makes cooperation plausible in this case? | `O-01`–`O-04` | Shared labels or stated trust do not establish communication, monitoring, or credible response |
| Can efficiency establish environmental improvement? | `CD-02`–`CD-04`; `L2-03` | Total throughput, ecological conditions, or rebound may alter the interpretation |

Consult the [course analysis](../cross-course/course-to-research.md) for locators and boundaries. Applications to data centers are our synthesis.

## A five-minute defense rehearsal

| Time | Task |
| :--- | :--- |
| 1 minute | State the question, decision, authority, place, and period |
| 1 minute | Explain one course concept that changes the analysis |
| 1 minute | Trace the strongest supporting claim to its source |
| 1 minute | Give the strongest rival explanation and a discriminating observation |
| 1 minute | State what is still unknown and which next record matters most |

These are internal practice timings. They do not replace the presentation assignment's brief.

<details>
<summary>Challenge questions for a second round</summary>

1. Which part of your explanation is descriptive, causal, or normative?
2. Why is this the right decision boundary, and what would a different boundary omit?
3. What record establishes that the decision-maker could access the evidence then?
4. Whose experience is missing, and how could you obtain it without treating one speaker as representative?
5. Could a favorable result arise without the claimed governance mechanism?
6. Which number changes most under a plausible alternative assumption?
7. What conclusion must remain withheld after your preferred next source arrives?
8. Which sentence requires personal knowledge that only you can supply?

</details>

Record actual revisions in the [learning log](learning-log.md). If an answer relies on an uncertain passage, carry that uncertainty into the discussion.
