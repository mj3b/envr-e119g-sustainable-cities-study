## What a contribution would need to demonstrate

| Possible contribution | Required work | Current verdict |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Original empirical finding | A consequential observation from a specific decision, interpreted against the closest literature | Open; no novelty claim yet |
| Useful synthesis | Show how authority, distribution and ecological constraints change the interpretation of the same decision | Feasible for Assignment 1; substantive human review pending |
| Methodological improvement | Demonstrate that the traceable workflow detects meaningful errors or supports better judgments than a defined comparison | Proposed; software tests alone do not show research benefit |
| Replication or extension | Reproduce or challenge an existing result in a justified new setting | Potentially valuable; requires explicit comparison and appropriate evidence |

The strongest prospective contribution is an auditable reconstruction of **assumptions → evidence access before authorization → enforceable conditions → monitoring → later observations and rival explanations**. The chain's usefulness must be demonstrated in the records. Its wording or diagram alone is not a novel method.

## Keep tomorrow's ambition proportionate

Assignment 1 can establish a serious problem, a defensible question, relevant course reasoning and a feasible learning agenda. It need not announce a new theory or prove a causal effect. Present originality as a question to investigate: what might this case reveal that the closest studies have not established?

Before a later novelty claim, read the closest studies in full, examine their cited and citing work, compare questions, units, methods and findings, and record both overlap and remaining uncertainty. An unsuccessful search for an identical study is not proof of originality.
