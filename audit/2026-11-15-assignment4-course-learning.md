## What changed in the argument

North now connects public information to authority and enforcement. Satterthwaite identifies outcome boundaries and the risk of neglecting problems with fewer data. Sen remains the primary account of public reasoning about development criteria. Lowe supplies the distinction between policy content and implementation. The Rio study adds physical inspection, residents’ accounts and maintenance over time. Gamperl contributes communication and coordination requirements. Blackman and Hoffmann inform how to interpret a trial combining a tool with assistance.

These roles are distributed through the memo. They are not seven competing theories given equal weight. The new synthesis distinguishes finding an answer, judging the adequacy of its criteria and obtaining an institutional response. Each requires different evidence. A usable register is not treated as proof of environmental improvement.

The actual submitted first assignment used North, Sen and Costanza–Daly to frame long-lived water commitments. Assignment 2 narrowed the question using North, Satterthwaite and Sen, with Lowe as a methodological precedent and Amazonia as context. This revision restores the visibility of that learning while incorporating later course methods. It does not invent field experience or claim completed trials.
