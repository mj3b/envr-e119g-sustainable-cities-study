# Ostrom: conditional cooperation in commons dilemmas

[Class overview](../README.md) · [Argument](argument-map.md) · [Questions](questions.md)

## Analytical use

### 1. Define the shared resource

An electricity grid, a watershed, atmospheric capacity and a privately owned facility are different analytical objects. Our application begins by identifying resource use, access, exclusion and the boundary of effects. Calling every infrastructure problem a commons dilemma would conceal the relevant interdependence.

### 2. Observe the mechanism in operation

`O-02` directs attention to observable adherence and feasible consequences. A monitoring requirement establishes a declared control. Operational evidence must show who measured what, whether a breach was detected, and whether the responsible actor could respond. These are our evidentiary questions, not a universal institutional recipe.

### 3. Account for context and power

The interactions in `O-03` and conditionality in `O-04` make policy transfer an empirical question. Cooperation may look different when participants have unequal authority or when nonparticipants bear costs. This motivates examining representation, exit options and enforcement asymmetry; it does not claim the paper measures those factors in any candidate place.

## What remains open

Neither universal commons failure nor universal community success follows from the inspected claims. A cooperative arrangement may remain physically inadequate or distribute burdens unequally. Those tests draw on Costanza and Daly and on the lecture’s distributional questions. Full working-paper coverage, chapter comparison and human review remain open.
