# Sen: plural evaluation and human development

Journal of Human Development 1(1), 17–23 (2000). The uploaded PDF contains a cover page; PDF page 2 is printed page 17. Extracted text has joined words, so quotations require visual verification.

## Argument and interpretation

Sen's essay asks how human development should be evaluated after a decade of work in the field. The inspected sections resist treating one measure as the whole object of evaluation. Income matters, but it cannot by itself settle questions about human well-being (S-01, PDF pages 5–6, printed 20–21).

The same warning applies to the Human Development Index. Its usefulness does not mean the broader human-development inquiry should collapse into an aggregate score (S-02, PDF page 7, printed 22). This is a challenge to how measures are used, not a reason to discard measurement. A table of several indicators still needs an account of why those dimensions matter and how disagreements will be handled.

Evaluative weights require reasoned public discussion (S-03, PDF page 6). In a city case, choosing employment, revenue, resource demand, access, or health as the outcome already reflects a judgment. The analyst should make that judgment visible and ask whose concerns the chosen measures leave out. That case application is our interpretation, not an empirical result from this essay.
