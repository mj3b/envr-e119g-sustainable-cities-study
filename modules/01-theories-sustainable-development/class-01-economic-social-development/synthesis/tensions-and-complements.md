# Class 01 · tensions worth testing

[Class overview](../README.md) · [North–Sen comparison](north-sen-comparison.md)

The value of a comparison is the question it sharpens. The tensions below are our synthesis of inspected claims; none establishes a local finding.

| Tension | Why it matters | Evidence that could change the interpretation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Institutional stability and desirable outcomes | Predictable rules may help coordination while distributing benefits and burdens unevenly. | Affected-group outcomes and reasons for the rule, alongside `N-02` (PDF p. 2) and `S-01` (PDF pp. 5–6). |
| Historical constraint and current feasibility | Persistence may reflect inherited switching costs, present conditions, or both. | Dated commitments, feasible alternatives and accounts of rejection; `N-03` (PDF pp. 2, 5), `L1-02` (L1C 14:22–17:14). |
| Plural evaluation and decision tractability | More dimensions improve scrutiny only if their relevance and tradeoffs are clear. | An omitted consequence that changes the comparison, or evidence that it does not; `S-02` (PDF p. 7). |
| Public discussion and actual influence | Reasons can be aired without changing whose interests govern the decision. | Timing of disclosure, responses to objections, representation and amendments; application of `S-03` (PDF p. 6). |

## Complementarity without forced agreement

North supplies a way to investigate why an arrangement persists. Sen supplies a way to question the adequacy of the outcome standard. Neither inquiry replaces the other. A well-explained harmful result remains harmful under the stated standard; a desirable result may still have an uncertain causal explanation.

## Disagreement to preserve

The peer assertion that change occurs only from below (`P1-02`, L1E 02:58–03:56) is stronger than the evidence coded from North. Keep it as a hypothesis. A case could examine how formal reform, enforcement and changes in shared beliefs interact without assuming one direction of causation in advance.
