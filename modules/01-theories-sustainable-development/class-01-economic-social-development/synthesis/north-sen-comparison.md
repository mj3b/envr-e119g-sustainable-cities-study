# North and Sen · explanation and evaluation

[Class overview](../README.md) · [Concept map](concept-map.md) · [Urban application](urban-application.md)

> **Synthesis `SYN-01`:** explaining how a development choice arose and evaluating its consequences require connected but distinct arguments.

## What each reading contributes

| Question | North | Sen |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Central task in the inspected claims | Explain institutional incentives and change over time. | Evaluate development through plural concerns and public reasoning. |
| Unit to make visible | Rules, informal constraints and enforcement (`N-02`, PDF p. 2). | Dimensions and evaluative weights (`S-01`, PDF pp. 5–6; `S-03`, p. 6). |
| Main analytical hazard | Naming an institution without demonstrating its influence. | Treating an aggregate indicator as the whole development judgment. |
| What the reading does not settle | Whether an observed institution produces a desirable distribution. | Which institutional mechanism caused a particular local result. |

## A connected research argument

1. Identify the decision and the actor’s authority.
2. Reconstruct how rules and inherited commitments affected the alternatives.
3. Identify the consequences that matter and the populations that experience them.
4. Examine who selected the measures and how objections were handled.
5. Separate uncertainty about what happened from disagreement about how to value it.

These steps are our methodological adaptation. `SYN-01` depends on `N-01`, `S-01`, `L1-04` and `L1-05`; it does not prove a relationship in any candidate city.

## A demanding counterargument

A decision may be institutionally predictable and defensible on several development dimensions. The analysis must allow that possibility. It should search for an omitted consequence or excluded alternative because it could matter, not presume that every infrastructure approval conceals failure.
