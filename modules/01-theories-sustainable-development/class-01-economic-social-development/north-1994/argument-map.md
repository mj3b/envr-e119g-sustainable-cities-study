# Argument reconstruction: North

1. Rules and their enforcement structure incentives (N-02).
2. Incentives shape the options and behavior through which economic activity proceeds (N-01).
3. Learning and inherited arrangements influence institutional evolution, which need not improve performance (N-03).
4. Therefore, explaining a development trajectory requires investigating institutions and their history alongside current technical conditions.

The fourth step is our reconstruction of the inspected argument. It does not identify an estimated effect size. For an urban application, the warrant is that a rule actually constrains the relevant choice. Evidence of an old rule alone cannot establish that warrant.

A competing explanation is that the observed choice follows current resource availability or demand even under different rules. Compare the documented alternatives, technical constraints, costs of switching, and accounts of who opposed change. A record showing that the purported institutional constraint was irrelevant would weaken the proposed mechanism.

Explanatory success does not settle normative evaluation. A stable arrangement can produce predictable benefits for one group and burdens for another. Sen and the lecture's distributional questions supply a separate evaluative inquiry.
