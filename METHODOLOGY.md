# Preserve the chain from source to application

The recovered architecture is source → provenance → claims → concepts → argument structure → lecture alignment → synthesis → urban application → assignment relevance → human review. Classes 1 and 2 jointly calibrate the system. AART contributes traceability and review discipline; this repository is a separate course system.

G1, source fidelity: every source-derived claim must trace to the actual supplied text and edition, with a usable page or timestamp. Inspect support, qualifications, and scope; a valid locator alone cannot establish substantive support.

G2, lecture fidelity: preserve the instructor’s important definitions, examples, qualifications, applications, and emphases. Audit the whole supplied lecture for omissions. Segment-specific speaker roles and transcription uncertainty remain explicit. Missing material blocks a claim of full coverage.

G3, synthesis fidelity: distinguish author argument, instructor framing, TA guidance, student contribution, external evidence, and our inference. Check dependencies and alternative interpretations. Peer comments can generate hypotheses; they do not become established findings by repetition.

Each gate needs a recorded human reviewer and rationale. Pending is not pass. The promotion tool refuses a master brief until all three gates pass, blocking omissions are resolved, and all included claims have approved human review. Automated structural validation never sets these approvals.

The reading–lecture matrix classifies alignment, extension, tension, lecture-only application, and peer lead. The emphasis map uses explicit pedagogical treatment, not unmeasured word frequency. Divergences remain visible even when they are complementary interpretations. Omissions records identify absent sources, incomplete coding, edition issues, and uncertainties.

A changing source invalidates its digest. Re-ingestion requires rechecking dependent claims and returning affected gates to pending. Keep earlier decisions and the rationale for corrections in version control. Do not silently overwrite an interpretation with an apparently stronger claim.
