# The Dalles: reconstructing a decision without inventing its outcome

**Status:** demonstration of the method; candidate remains unselected. Human review pending.

The council record supports a dated authorization and competing positions. It does not yet support a conclusion about subsequent water security. The narrow question is which assumptions supported the authorization, who could examine their basis, and what later observations could test them (`RQ-DAL`).

## Test two explanations

One hypothesis is that a verifiable operating condition leads to monitoring, detection, and correction (`H-01`). Its rival is that an apparent improvement comes from changed operating demand, weather, or infrastructure, independently of enforcement (`H-02`). A time series and a signed agreement alone cannot separate these explanations.

Retrieve the executed agreement and studies first. Identify a measurable trigger, the actor with power to respond, and the relevant meter and aquifer boundaries. Then seek monitoring and response records before selecting comparable periods. Preserve changes in other users’ demand, weather, and supply as possible explanations.

A request to defer is recorded as a contemporaneous proposed alternative. Phasing service around monitored thresholds is separately labeled as an analyst proposal. The latter requires legal and engineering feasibility checks before it can be treated as an option available to the council.

## What this example teaches

The number discrepancy is an unresolved within-source inconsistency, not evidence of deception. The absence of a retrieved meter series is missing research evidence, not a zero outcome. The recorded authorization is an output. An assessment of physical consequences remains open.
