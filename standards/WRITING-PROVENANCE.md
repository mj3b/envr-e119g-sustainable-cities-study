## What the adaptation preserves

The packet distinguishes material it labels recovered, supported synthesis, derived examples, and items needing verification or source recovery. Those are the packet’s own provenance claims. Inspection of the packet does not independently authenticate the original classroom material.

Its open-question section says the exact grammatical order of operations, several classroom definitions, the original period-test procedure, the complete discouraged-pattern inventory, and the `Voice-Register-System.txt` taxonomy remain unrecovered. This revision retains those gaps. The new register names, structure, and hypothetical examples are local AI-assisted editorial synthesis for this repository.

The previous standard named *Effective Writing: A Working System*, *Anti-AI Writing System*, *Academic Writing*, and *Voice Register System* as inputs inspected during earlier work. That historical description remains in Git history. Those four original files were not freshly inspected for this revision, so their exact contents and completeness are not newly certified.

Mark’s constraints on punctuation, tone, first person, and formatting are house preferences. They are kept separate from grammatical requirements and from factual or attribution safeguards. A preference cannot turn a passive construction into a grammar error or justify changing an unknown actor into a named one.

## Publication and review

The raw attachment remains outside the public repository. This note records its identity, bounded use, and unresolved provenance. The standard paraphrases selected ideas and supplies separately labelled examples rather than republishing the teaching packet.

AI authored the revised standard and its explanatory examples under Mark’s instructions. Human review of the resulting voice specification remains separate from maintenance authorization. The revision introduces no new scholarly theory, research finding, institutional endorsement, or claim that the named source materials are complete.
