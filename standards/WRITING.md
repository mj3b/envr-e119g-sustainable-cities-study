# Mark’s Writing Rules and Voice Register System

[Home](../README.md) / Standards / Writing · [Editorial provenance](WRITING-PROVENANCE.md) · [Markdown presentation](MARKDOWN.md)

Write so a reader can identify the problem, follow the reasoning, and judge what the evidence permits. Mark’s default voice is direct, analytically precise, and restrained. Authority comes from a defensible claim and an intelligible explanation. Complexity belongs where the subject requires it.

This is a personal editorial standard for authored prose in this repository. The audience and assignment determine the register. Source quotations, official titles, identifiers, and deliberately preserved historical text retain their wording. The provenance note separates supplied preferences, reconstructed teaching material, and the local editorial synthesis introduced here.

## 1. Establish the writing brief

Before drafting, identify the reader, purpose, question or decision, permitted claim, length, and required form. Record material constraints such as citation style, first-person conventions, and confidentiality. A short note may need only one sentence of planning. A research memorandum needs a bounded question and a reason to investigate it.

Open with the actual problem or finding. Introduce background when the reader needs it to interpret the evidence. Choose theories because they change a question, explanation, or evaluation criterion. Naming everything read gives the reader an inventory instead of an argument.

Apply requirements in this order: factual and attribution integrity; the current assignment or commissioned brief; the conventions of the selected register; these house preferences. Resolve a material conflict explicitly. A preference for active sentences never licenses an invented actor.

## 2. Keep the voice recognizable

Use concrete nouns and verbs that identify the action. Name who decided, what changed, which condition mattered, and who could experience the consequence when the evidence establishes those facts. Retain a necessary technical term and explain it at first use. Replace jargon that contributes no precision.

Take a position at the strength the evidence supports. A directly inspected record warrants a clear statement about its contents. A plausible explanation requires an identified inference and the uncertainty that limits it. Avoid both unsupported certainty and strings of tentative words that obscure a well-supported point.

Use first person when it identifies responsibility, a research choice, or an interpretation that the author actually owns. Address the reader when doing so serves the task. In candidate drafts, distinguish a proposed first-person sentence from an action Mark has confirmed. Never invent a memory, feeling, credential, realization, or change of mind.

Vary sentence length and structure with the work each sentence performs. Use a short sentence to establish a boundary or decision. Give a complex relationship enough room to remain accurate. Connected prose should carry the reasoning without either a succession of fragments or a chain of overfilled sentences.

## 3. Separate grammar from editorial preference

Assess each revision on four grounds: grammatical structure, stylistic choice, rhetorical effect, and evidentiary consequence. A grammatical sentence can remain vague or misleading. A house preference is a contextual editorial choice, not a universal prohibition.

Find the finite verb and its subject before polishing the sentence. Check agreement and identify the independent and dependent clauses. Repair fused clauses, comma splices, and unintended fragments with punctuation or a conjunction that expresses the intended relationship. Sentence length alone does not diagnose a run-on.

Use subordination deliberately. *Because* presents a reason or cause; *after* locates events in time; *although* introduces a concession; *if* states a condition. Replacing one with another can change the claim. A smoother sentence must preserve the relationship the evidence supports.

Place modifiers beside their intended targets. Make pronoun reference unambiguous. Repeat a necessary noun when *this*, *it*, or *they* could refer to several things. Give genuinely equivalent items parallel form; do not manufacture conceptual equivalence to obtain a balanced sentence.

Prefer active voice when the actor is known and relevant. Preserve passive voice when the actor is unknown, the recipient of an action is the appropriate focus, or another construction would distort attribution. Move substantive action into the verb when that improves clarity. Retain a nominalization when it names a needed concept or connects one sentence to the next.

Place important conditions and consequences where the reader can connect them to the correct proposition. Inspect the sentence ending for misplaced emphasis. Shortening a sentence must preserve agency, scope, negation, modality, and time.

## 4. Match each claim to its evidence

Develop the claim through a source observation, the reasoning that connects the observation to the claim, and any qualification that affects its use. That connecting explanation is the warrant. Make it visible without turning every paragraph into an identical four-part template.

Attribute source statements precisely. Identify the author, institution, document, or dataset that supplies the evidence. Cite the inspected version and the passage relevant to the sentence. A report-level reference cannot support a claim absent from that report. Introduce quotations, interpret them, and explain their bearing on the inquiry.

Keep observation, attributed assertion, interpretation, causal explanation, and normative judgment distinguishable. Use *reports*, *records*, *projects*, *argues*, and *estimates* according to the source’s actual function. A court’s account of another filing supports an attributed description; it does not establish direct inspection of that filing.

For quantities, retain units, denominator, period, boundary, comparator, and uncertainty as applicable. Identify whether a number is a right, capacity, peak design demand, model input, forecast, permitted limit, installed quantity, or measured observation. A flow rate becomes annual use only when the operating assumptions support that calculation.

State the strongest relevant alternative accurately. Explain whether it changes the conclusion, limits confidence, or remains unresolved. Identify the evidence that could change the assessment. A confidence label needs an evidentiary reason. Neither model confidence nor agreement among models supplies a missing source.

## 5. Build paragraphs that advance the inquiry

Give each substantive paragraph a clear job: establish a fact, develop an interpretation, explain a mechanism, test an alternative, or delimit an uncertainty. A paragraph may do more than one job when the relationship is explicit. Put its controlling point early enough for the reader to understand why the evidence follows.

Make transitions name the relationship between ideas. A repeated key term can provide continuity; a generic connector cannot repair an unexplained logical jump. Define technical concepts consistently across the document. Vary ordinary phrasing without treating authorization, implementation, and outcome as interchangeable synonyms.

End a paragraph where it has added something: a consequence, a limit, a reason for the next step, or a question the evidence makes answerable. Remove sentences that merely announce importance. Close the document with the warranted implication or a concrete research action. Avoid a ceremonial recap that restates every section.

## 6. Choose a register for the reader

Voice remains recognizable across contexts. Register changes vocabulary, sentence density, explanation, formality, and the placement of evidence. The following register names are local editorial tools, not a recovered classroom taxonomy.

| Register | Reader’s need | Writing choices |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Scholarly and analytical | Inspect an argument and its limits | Define contested terms, show the warrant, cite the precise source, and address credible alternatives. Use first person where the brief permits and attribution benefits. |
| Technical and evidentiary | Reproduce a procedure or check a result | Name inputs, versions, units, conditions, outputs, failures, and inspection scope. Separate specifications from observations. |
| Policy and decision | Understand a decision, feasible action, and its consequences | Lead with the decision problem; identify authority, affected groups, alternatives, implementation constraints, and evidence that remains missing. |
| Professional correspondence | Understand the purpose and respond appropriately | State the reason for writing and the requested action early. Use concise paragraphs and a respectful, proportionate tone. Add headings only when they help navigation. |
| Public explanation | Understand a consequential issue without specialist knowledge | Define essential terms through concrete examples. Keep the mechanism and qualification intact while reducing technical density. |
| Reflective and personal | Understand the author’s actual experience or intellectual choices | Distinguish experience, values, interpretation, and external fact. Let Mark confirm the personal account and any reported learning. |
| Spoken explanation | Follow an argument on one hearing | Use short signposts, pronounceable sentences, and a selective evidence sequence. Read aloud and measure delivery time. |

An executive summary may compress the evidence discussion, but it must retain the limitation that could change the decision. A public explanation may replace a specialist term, but it must preserve the concept. A personal reflection may be candid without claiming experiences the author has never supplied.

## 7. Remove mechanical prose

Delete empty openings, redundant qualifications, self-praise, and commentary about the quality of the answer. Replace promotional descriptions with the procedure, result, or limit that a reader can inspect. Avoid stock phrasing that could introduce almost any subject.

Do not use em dashes in newly authored prose under this house standard. Avoid formulaic contrast frames, question-and-answer theatrics, repeated rhetorical questions, and habitual three-item constructions. A necessary negative or a genuine three-part classification should retain its meaning; rewrite the presentation when it has become mechanical. Preserve these forms in exact quotations and required identifiers.

Audit repeated sentence frames and decorative abstractions. Words Mark has flagged, including *sharpened* and *distinction*, should not become routine transitions. Repetition can still be necessary for technical consistency. Treat a word list as a diagnostic aid rather than an invitation to substitute imprecise synonyms.

Use headings to expose the document’s structure. Use tables for comparisons and lists for genuinely parallel items or an ordered procedure. Avoid bold-titled bullet after bullet when connected paragraphs would explain the relationship better. Follow the separate Markdown standard for presentation.

## 8. Preserve meaning while changing register

The following sentences are newly authored editorial exercises. They are hypothetical and provide no findings about Cerrillos or any other project.

**Underlying evidence:** a supplied report projects lower water use under a revised design. The packet contains no post-implementation measurements.

Academic: “The report’s projected reduction supports a design-stage claim; the supplied record cannot establish realized water savings.”

Technical: “Record the value as a forecast. Post-implementation measurements are unavailable in the supplied packet.”

Policy: “Treat the projected reduction as a design assumption when evaluating the proposal. Assess realized savings only when comparable operating measurements become available.”

Public: “The report estimates that the revised design would use less water. The records supplied do not show how much water it used in operation.”

The form changes. The evidentiary status stays fixed. None of these versions claims that the project operated or that measurements are unavailable everywhere.

A second exercise concerns agency. “The permit was withdrawn” cannot become “Residents forced the company to surrender its permit” unless evidence supports the new actors, action, and causal explanation. Preserve the smaller claim while the causal record remains unresolved.

## 9. Revise from the argument to the sentence

Use separate passes so a surface improvement cannot conceal a substantive change.

1. **Brief and structure.** Check the actual assignment, reader, question, and length. Give every section a necessary function and reorder it for the reader.
2. **Evidence and reasoning.** Verify the claims actually used, the inspected versions, their locators, the warrants, and the strongest alternatives. Remove or narrow unsupported claims.
3. **Grammar and reference.** Check sentence cores, agreement, clause boundaries, modifiers, pronouns, parallelism, and attribution.
4. **Register and rhythm.** Adjust technical density, first person, transitions, repetition, punctuation, and sentence length. Read aloud. Remove padding while preserving useful explanation.
5. **Integrity and delivery.** Compare the revision with the evidence and earlier wording. Recheck numbers, citation coverage, limits, AI disclosure, word count, and artifact status. Obtain the required human review before submission.

Apply each pass only where it is relevant. Record unresolved questions rather than forcing a pass. A style edit that strengthens a factual claim requires renewed source review; a register change does not supply that review.

## 10. Record editorial assistance honestly

State where AI helped retrieve, classify, interpret, translate, draft, critique, or revise material. Separate those activities from deterministic file comparisons and tests. Report human checks only when a person actually performed them on the identified version. Avoid attributing all work to “editing” when AI generated the argument or prose.

Neither can approve Mark’s voice, validate a legal interpretation, or replace original-source inspection.

## Acceptance boundary

Before sharing a finished artifact, a reader should be able to recover its question, bounded answer, supporting evidence, inferential steps, and consequential limits. The language should fit the reader and preserve the author’s actual position. Mark must be able to explain and defend any first-person claim presented as his own.

A linter can identify a pattern, and a parser can check a structure. Neither establishes sound grammar in every context, faithful interpretation, scholarly originality, or authorship. This standard guides the work; the artifact and its review record show what was accomplished.
