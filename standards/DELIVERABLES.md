# From research packet to deliverable

Every deliverable begins with its verified assignment brief, intended audience, central question, and authorized scope. Assignment 1 remains paused. Future outputs should be authored from selected evidence, with enough context to be understood without this repository.

## Research packet

A ready packet contains the bounded question; user-owned reflection if required; a small set of relevant concepts; claim-to-source mapping; a decision timeline; credible alternatives; counterevidence; known omissions; and the conclusion the evidence permits. Identify each output paragraph or slide's supporting claim IDs and locators in a companion evidence map. Mark normative judgments and personal recollections separately.

Do not make every assignment depend on resolving every semester uncertainty. Approve only the claims and scope actually used. A class master brief requires full declared-scope coverage; a narrow assignment packet can disclose the limits of the inspected subset. It still requires human verification of its used claims. This distinction prevents an unresolved edition mapping from being silently ignored or from unnecessarily preventing an appropriately qualified question.

## Memo and blog workflow

Prepare an argument outline after drafting is authorized. Each section has a purpose, evidence, inference, and transition. A personal opening must establish an actual stake. Analysis must show why the issue is urban: infrastructure and place, governing authority, distribution, ecological conditions, and feasible action. The ending identifies consequential learning questions or warranted implications. Apply E5 editing, check word count according to the prompt, then verify all citations and the AI acknowledgment.

## Presentation workflow

Build a storyboard before slides. Use one central claim per slide; choose evidence or a diagram that makes that claim easier to assess. A slide record includes title, purpose, evidence IDs, visual source, spoken explanation, limitation, and allotted seconds. Speaker notes carry detail the audience cannot read during delivery. Label conceptual diagrams as conceptual. Data graphics need units, dates, boundaries, sources, and uncertainty.

For five minutes, start with a provisional five-part sequence: question/place (40 seconds), urban stakes (60), theory and evidence (90), interpretation and limitation (70), implication/question (40). For eight minutes, add comparison, alternatives, and a learning trajectory. These timings are planning suggestions; the assignment governs. Rehearse aloud and revise to the actual measured duration.

Render every slide and inspect it at presentation size. Check clipping, readable labels, contrast, source placement, and the sequence without narration. Reopen the exported deck or PDF to verify that it matches the inspected version. Keep source materials private and store exported deliverables locally until their contents and sharing scope are reviewed.

## Release record

Record the exact artifact version, prompt version, evidence map, word count or rehearsal duration, unresolved limitations, Mark's review, and submission status. Never label work submitted merely because an artifact exists. The course permits AI with acknowledgment and student responsibility (SYL page 7; TA-03). No automatic workflow can certify that Mark understands or endorses an argument.
