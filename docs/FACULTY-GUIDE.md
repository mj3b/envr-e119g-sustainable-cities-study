## How the course changes the question

North directs attention to rules, enforcement, and inherited arrangements. Sen’s assigned essay makes evaluative dimensions and weights contestable. Nora’s capabilities framing adds attention to what residents can actually do. Ostrom makes cooperation conditional on context, trust, monitoring, and credible responses. Costanza and Daly require explicit ecological stocks, flows, and constraints.

Those contributions remain distinct. The [course bridge](../cross-course/course-to-research.md) gives supporting claim IDs and locators, then labels the case applications as our synthesis. It records where an attractive application could overreach.

## How to inspect a claim

Start with an ID in a case brief or the course bridge. The [claim registry](../cross-course/claims.json) contains the exact paraphrase, evidence class, source, locator, limitations, and human-review status. The [source registry](../cross-course/evidence-registry.json) identifies the edition or public URL. The [research objects](../cases/RESEARCH-OBJECTS.md) show how those claims support a question, hypothesis, assumption, or reconstruction.

Public-source links lead to external records. Course-source locators refer to privately held course copies. The repository contains authored notes and short paraphrases; it does not distribute those readings or transcripts.
